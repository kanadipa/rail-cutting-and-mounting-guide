# Cabinets 101

Everything you need to know about electrical cabinets for this challenge. You do not need to know
any electrical engineering.

## The cabinet

A **control cabinet** (German: _Schaltschrank_) is a steel box that holds the electrical brain of a
machine: power distribution, fuses, relays, a PLC (the industrial computer that runs the machine),
and hundreds of wire connections.

Inside, at the back, is a removable steel sheet called the **mounting plate** (_Montageplatte_).
Almost everything is fixed to it, most of it on rails.

## DIN rails

A **DIN rail** (_Tragschiene_, _Hutschiene_) is a standard metal profile that components clip
onto. Rails are bought as **raw bars**, and each rail on the plate is **cut to its length** from
one. One bar can give several pieces.

## Components on a rail

Components clip onto the rail from the front. They sit side by side, touching, from left to right.

## BMK: the device tag

Every rail and every component has a **BMK** (_Betriebsmittelkennzeichen_, "equipment
identifier"), such as `-U1`, `-2K1` or `-XD1:5`. It is printed on a label on the part.
**It is how we identify components.**

## The work at the bench

1. **Cutting.** The operator takes a raw bar, measures and cuts the piece, then repeats for every
   rail.
2. **Mounting.** The operator takes one cut rail, then picks each component from the shelf and
   clips it on, from left to right in the planned order, until the rail is full. Then the next
   rail.

The finished rails are later screwed onto the mounting plate and wired.

Mistakes are expensive. A part in the wrong place or order means taking parts off again. The
screen's job is to make the right next action obvious.
